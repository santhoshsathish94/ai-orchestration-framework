Plain-language definitions

Clover and the four stages

Clover
This framework. An AI Orchestration Framework connecting real-world Context, human Direction, AI-driven Action, and validated Success into a repeatable cycle. Four stages, describing how human intent becomes an outcome the environment confirms, and what the next attempt starts from.
Stage
One part of the cycle. Each stage has one job, and there are four. In the clover mark each stage is drawn as a leaf.
The working loop
Context, Direction, Action, Success. The four stages a team runs, over and over.
Context
Everything the work reasons from, and the stage the cycle starts in. In the common clover it is only what one human can hand over — what they type, the files they attach, the repository they happen to be working in. In the lucky clover it is the systems the organization already runs: the repositories and their documentation, the datasources, the logs and telemetry, the deployment environments, the running applications.
Direction
The human controls what matters, the desired outcome, constraints, boundaries, and what must not happen, and approves. With real context available, Direction also points at where the answer probably is.
Action
AI determines how the work should happen and executes within those boundaries: planning, tool choice, orchestration across agents, changes, tests, debugging.
Success
The intended outcome demonstrated by the real environment. A closed task, a passing build, or a confident report sits outside this.
Growth
The next stage, and not one of the four. AI becoming more capable out of the four stages: patterns form, and expertise forms out of the patterns. No team runs it, because it belongs to the frontier AI companies and the volume of data everyone's usage generates. The growth clover.
The common clover
Three stages — Direction, Action, Success. How AI is used almost everywhere today.
The lucky clover
Four stages, with Context added and running first. This is the framework, and the state we are in now. The lucky clover.
The growth clover
The four stages with Growth drawn beside them as the next stage. It happens whether anyone chooses it or not. Growth.

Everything else

Orchestration
Coordinating people, AI, tools, and context so that work produces an outcome the environment confirms, rather than each part doing its own thing.
Orchestration environment
The access layer between AI and the systems an organization already uses. Read-only MCP servers in front of the repositories, datasources, logs and environments. It is what feeds the Context stage.
Capability
Anything that can do work: a human, a team, an AI, an agent, a tool, a system.
Intent
What a human actually wants to achieve. The outcome, and not the task.
Outcome
The change in the real world that was wanted. Distinct from output, which is what got produced.
Evidence
What was actually done to check a claim — an assertion, one manual look, a repeatable test, a before-and-after measurement, or the original signal gone from the real environment. Say which. More on Success.
Experience
What was learned from one cycle — what was tried, what happened, what the evidence showed.
Expertise
A reusable pattern that emerges from several validated experiences. One cycle is not expertise.
Ownership
The named human accountable for an outcome. Work can be delegated; ownership cannot.
Agent
An AI system that can take actions and use tools, rather than only produce text.
Agentic workflow
A designed loop of agent steps that repeats a known process. Orchestration differs in that it keeps what the outcome taught it.
Autonomy
How much of the path AI is trusted to determine — widened where results have held, capped by blast radius, granted per context rather than globally. Widening what AI decides.
Telemetry
The signals a running system emits about itself — logs, metrics, traces, error rates.
Blast radius
How much damage a change could do if it is wrong. A bigger blast radius means more human approval.
Non-production
Any environment that is not serving real users — local, test, staging. Safe to break.
Guardrail
A constraint that keeps work inside safe boundaries: a required approval, a scope limit, a check that must pass.
Root cause
The underlying reason a problem occurs. Distinct from the symptom, and from the workaround that hides it.
Workaround
Something that stops the pain without fixing the cause. Legitimate for stabilizing an incident, and not a destination.
Thrashing
Repeated confident attempts at a fix, none of which work. The signal to stop fixing and go back to Context.
